# STAR Presentation

## S - Situation

Students rely on part-time work to pay for tuition and living costs. Inflation
reduces their purchasing power, making essentials harder to afford even when
their hourly wage increases.

## T - Task

Answer the question: Have student wages kept up with inflation over the past 10
years? Viewers should be able to compare nominal and real wages, then decide
whether pay growth improved student purchasing power.

## A - Action

- Built an interactive chart comparing nominal wages with real wages.
- Added a year slider so the viewer can stop the trend at different points.
- Added a specific callout that reports nominal and real wage change through
  the selected year.
- Added an "Ask the data" chatbox that answers from the same processed dataset
  instead of exposing an API key in the browser.
- Processed wage and CPI data into a predictable year-by-year dataset.

## R - Result

- Real wages have remained mostly flat despite nominal wage increases.
- From 2013 to 2022, nominal wages rose from $11.80 to $14.50, while real wages
  rose from $10.20 to $10.80.
- The interaction shows that inflation erodes much of the apparent wage gain.
- Limitation: The current data uses U.S. averages and may not represent every
  student, region, or job type.
- Key takeaway: Students should compare wage increases with inflation before
  assuming they can afford more.
